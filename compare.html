Every pick weighs the same, so a feature you care about more deserves a look at the text under it.

What each kind of app is for

Hevy and Strong are workout logs. They record every set of the program you bring and list no calorie target. Fitbod generates a different workout every session and lists no diet plan. Liftoff also builds sessions, then scores each set from 1 to 1,000 and ranks you against friends, your country and the world, with a food log on the side and no protocol set by your body type. MacroFactor is a food log first, with training in a separate app and a separate subscription.

IronQuest follows one method for one goal, a V-shaped body: 3 sessions a week of 38 to 44 minutes with the weight of every set, a calorie and protein target set by your body type (build, recomp or cut) and XP that moves your rank from E to S. It runs on iPhone, with sessions that keep working offline, and every plan starts with 14 days free.

Questions

Which gym apps can you try for free?

All 6. Hevy, Strong and Liftoff have a free plan: Hevy with no ads, Strong with up to 3 custom routines and Liftoff for core workout tracking. Fitbod and MacroFactor start with a 7-day free trial, and IronQuest with 14 days free on every plan, then each needs a subscription.

Which gym app has training and nutrition in one app?

IronQuest and Liftoff both hold a training plan and calorie or macro targets in one app. MacroFactor splits them into two apps, MacroFactor for food and MacroFactor Workouts for training, each listed at $11.99 a month on the US App Store. Hevy, Strong and Fitbod list no nutrition feature.

Which of these apps run on Android?

Hevy, Strong, Fitbod, MacroFactor and Liftoff all have an Android version. IronQuest is built for iPhone, with the rest timer in the Dynamic Island and on the lock screen and sessions that keep working offline.
