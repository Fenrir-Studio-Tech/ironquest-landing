IronQuest vs MacroFactor

Pick MacroFactor if detailed food logging is the job: a large food database, AI photo and barcode logging, micronutrients and calorie targets recalculated every week from your intake and weight, on iPhone or Android. Pick IronQuest if you want the gym program and a simpler calorie and protein target in one app and one subscription, built for a V-shaped body in about 2 hours of gym a week.

How deep the food tracking goes

MacroFactor is the stronger food tracker. Its site lists a searchable database of over 1.36 million entries, AI photo logging, barcode and nutrition label scanning, speech-to-text, full micronutrient tracking, and diet styles from keto to carb-focused. You choose between Coached, Collaborative and Manual modes for a bulk, a cut or maintenance.

IronQuest keeps the diet to a few numbers. Your body type sets the protocol (build, recomp or cut), and you get a daily calorie target, a protein target and an eating window, 8 hours by default, on one dial that fills as you log a meal. The two targets IronQuest holds you to are calories and protein.

How the weekly adjustment works

MacroFactor calculates your energy expenditure from the food you log and the change in your body weight, then updates your macro targets every week. Its site says the calculation ignores smartwatch data and does not depend on how closely you followed the plan. The method works best when you log everything you eat.

IronQuest adjusts from a weekly check-in: one weigh-in and 4 tape measurements (belly, waist, shoulders, thigh). It compares your body with the 12-month projection and moves the daily target when your progress calls for it. The tape matters because the goal is a ratio, shoulders 1.618 times the waist.

Training

The MacroFactor nutrition app has no workouts. Training lives in MacroFactor Workouts, a second app with its own subscription at $11.99 a month, $47.99 every 6 months or $71.99 a year. It generates a personalized program, adjusts targets from your logged results with rule-based progression, and shares body metrics with the nutrition app. The Workouts page mentions a bundle of both apps for existing customers without giving its price, and the App Store lists an "Annual Subscription Bundle" at $89.99.

IronQuest holds both halves in one app. The program is 3 sessions a week (V-Taper 44 min, Iron Legs 43 min, Boulder Shoulders 38 min) with weight and reps on every set, and daily steps stand in for cardio.

Recipes

MacroFactor imports a recipe from a URL or from a photo of a cookbook page. IronQuest imports from TikTok: share a recipe video to IronQuest and it comes back with ingredients, steps, a photo, and kcal and protein per portion. IronQuest also ships a high-protein recipe library with a step-by-step cooking mode and timers.

The game layer

MacroFactor's site lists habit tracking for logging streaks and no other game feature. IronQuest pays XP for sessions, steps and quests, moves your rank from E to S, keeps a weekly streak with freezes, grades 59 achievement cards from E to S and dresses a 3D character in outfits you earn by training.

Pick MacroFactor if

  • You want to log every food precisely, with a large database, barcode scanning and AI photo logging.
  • You track micronutrients or follow a specific diet style such as keto or low-carb.
  • You already have a training program and only need the nutrition side.
  • You use an Android phone.
  • You want targets calculated from your logged intake and weight every week.

Pick IronQuest if

  • You want the gym program and the diet in one app and one subscription.
  • A calorie target, a protein target and an eating window are enough tracking for you.
  • Your goal is a V-shaped body, and you want the tape measure to steer the plan.
  • You find recipes on TikTok and want their kcal and protein per portion.
  • You want ranks, quests and a streak around the whole routine, training and steps included.

Questions about IronQuest and MacroFactor

How much does MacroFactor cost?

MacroFactor lists US prices of $11.99 a month, $47.99 every 6 months and $71.99 a year, after a 7-day free trial, with no free plan (checked 16 Nov 2026). MacroFactor Workouts is a separate app listed at the same prices.

Does MacroFactor include workouts?

The nutrition app does not. Training is in MacroFactor Workouts, a separate app on iOS and Android that generates a personalized program and has its own subscription.

Is IronQuest as detailed as MacroFactor for food tracking?

No. MacroFactor tracks micronutrients and logs food from a database of over 1.36 million entries. IronQuest steers you with a daily calorie target, a protein target and an eating window.

Can I use MacroFactor and IronQuest together?

You can, but you would pay two subscriptions and follow two calorie targets. If you want MacroFactor to run your diet, a plain workout log covers the gym side for less.

Is IronQuest on Android?

No. IronQuest runs on iPhone only. MacroFactor is on the App Store and Google Play.
