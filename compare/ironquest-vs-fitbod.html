IronQuest vs Fitbod

Pick Fitbod if you want AI-generated workouts built around your own goals, on iPhone, Android or Apple Watch, and you handle your diet elsewhere. Pick IronQuest if you want the same 3 sessions every week, built for a V-shaped body, with a calorie target and a rank system in the same app.

A generated workout or a fixed week

Fitbod's App Store description leads with AI-generated workouts, a plan customized around your goals, and non-linear periodization to keep progress going. The app builds the session for you each time you train.

IronQuest does not generate programs with AI. Every player follows the same 3 sessions: V-Taper (44 min), Iron Legs (43 min) and Boulder Shoulders (38 min). Your answers choose the exercises once (pull-ups or pulldowns, squat or leg press, a sensitive back or knees), each set shows its weight and reps, and you repeat those lifts week after week with more load.

One goal or any goal

Fitbod plans around the fitness goals you give it. IronQuest has a single target for everyone: shoulders 1.618 times the waist. It reads your height, weight and body type, shows the body you can reach in 12 months and gives you the waist and shoulder numbers to aim for.

If your goal is a bigger squat, general fitness or training at home with changing equipment, Fitbod covers it and IronQuest does not try to.

Where Fitbod is ahead

Fitbod has an Android app, an Apple Watch app and runs on Mac. IronQuest runs on iPhone only.

Fitbod connects to Strava and Fitbit as well as Apple Health. IronQuest syncs with Apple Health only.

Fitbod changes the workout for you, which suits a lifter who gets bored repeating the same sessions. IronQuest keeps the sessions fixed on purpose.

Diet and the game layer

Fitbod's App Store page lists no nutrition feature and no ranks or quests. IronQuest covers both. Your body type sets a protocol (build, recomp or cut) with a daily calorie and protein target, and a weekly check-in with a weigh-in and 4 tape measurements adjusts it. Sessions, steps and quests pay XP that moves your rank from E to S, with a weekly streak, 59 achievement cards and a 3D character whose outfits you earn by training.

Pick Fitbod if

  • You want the app to build each workout for you.
  • Your goal is something other than a V-shaped physique.
  • You train with an Android phone or want the workout on your Apple Watch.
  • You already track food in another app, or you do not want to track it.
  • You use Strava or Fitbit and want your training connected to them.

Pick IronQuest if

  • You want a fixed program you can learn and progress on: 3 sessions, 38 to 44 minutes each.
  • You are training for looks: broad shoulders, narrow waist.
  • You want training, calories, protein and recipes under one subscription.
  • XP, ranks E to S and quests make you more likely to show up.

Questions about IronQuest and Fitbod

Does IronQuest use AI to build workouts like Fitbod?

No. IronQuest gives every player the same 3-session program and fits the exercises to their answers. Fitbod describes its workouts as AI-generated on its App Store page.

How much does Fitbod cost?

Fitbod starts with 7 days free. The US App Store lists its Monthly Plan at $15.99 and $12.99 and a year of Fitbod Elite at $95.99 and $79.99 (checked 16 Nov 2026). The listing does not say which price a new subscriber pays.

Does Fitbod include a diet plan?

Fitbod's App Store page lists no nutrition feature (checked 16 Nov 2026). IronQuest includes a nutrition protocol with a daily calorie and protein target and recipes imported from TikTok.

Is IronQuest on Android or Apple Watch?

No. IronQuest runs on iPhone only. Fitbod has an Android listing on Google Play and an Apple Watch app.
