Should I bulk or cut at 20 % body fat?

At 20 % body fat, a man should lose fat before he bulks. IronQuest takes 625 kcal a day off maintenance at 20 to 25 % body fat, whether the plan is called a recomp or a cut. A 180 lb (81.6 kg) man at 20 % then reaches 15 % in about 15 weeks and 10 % in about 33 weeks, where the plan switches to a build.

By Priya RamanBody fat, calories and protein

Published November 22, 2026Updated November 22, 2026

Key facts

  • IronQuest applies a 625 kcal daily deficit to men between 20 and 25 % body fat.
  • A 180 lb (81.6 kg) man at 20 % body fat starts on 2,150 kcal a day.
  • At 625 kcal a day, the first weeks remove about 1.25 lb (0.57 kg) a week.
  • In Garthe's 2011 trial, athletes aiming to lose 0.7 % of body weight weekly gained 2.1 % lean mass.
  • In Forbes's 2000 review, 60 to 70 % of the weight thin people gained was lean tissue.

At 20 % body fat, lose fat first and build afterwards. IronQuest takes 625 kcal a day off a man's maintenance anywhere from 20 to 25 % body fat. A 180 lb (81.6 kg) man at 20 % who walks 7,500 to 9,999 steps a day maintains on about 2,750 kcal, so he eats 2,150 kcal. He reaches 15 % in about 15 weeks and 10 % in about 33 weeks. At 10 % the plan moves to maintenance calories and a build. Your starting body only decides whether IronQuest calls the plan a recomp or a cut.

Why not bulk at 20 % body fat?

A surplus at 20 % adds a larger share of fat than the same surplus on a lean body. Forbes reviewed overfeeding studies of 3 weeks or more in 2000, in the Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences. Thin people put on 60 to 70 % of the gained weight as lean tissue. People with obesity put on 30 to 40 %. The fatter the starting body, the smaller the lean share, and the same pattern held for weight lost.

IronQuest's rule goes the same way. Above 15 % body fat, no plan starts with a surplus. The deficit for each body-fat band runs from 875 kcal a day at 25 % and above down to 250 kcal under 12 %. The build begins at 10 %, at maintenance, and adds 200 kcal a day only after 2 weekly reviews without a gain, up to 600 kcal.

Does the verdict change if you have never trained?

The verdict stays the same, and the name changes: a man at 20 % who has never lifted runs a recomp. In IronQuest, he picks the skinny-fat silhouette at setup, which covers 18 to 25 % body fat. The recomp uses the same 625 kcal deficit as a cut, with 2.6 g of protein per kg of lean mass and 3 gym sessions a week.

The name says what to expect. A beginner can lose fat and add muscle in the same months, so his waist shrinks faster than his weight drops. Barakat and colleagues reviewed the recomposition research in 2020, in the Strength and Conditioning Journal. They note the common view that this only happens in untrained or overweight people. Their review then cites a large body of studies where it also happened in resistance-trained lifters.

What if you already train?

A trained man at 20 % runs a cut, which in IronQuest means the muscular-but-fat silhouette, covering 18 to 28 % body fat. The daily deficit is 625 kcal, the same as the recomp. The difference is the pace he can expect from the mirror: a trained body adds muscle more slowly, so more of the change shows on the scale.

The rate of loss matters for a trained lifter who wants to keep his muscle. Garthe and colleagues tested 2 rates on 24 elite athletes in 2011, in the International Journal of Sport Nutrition and Exercise Metabolism. Both groups lost about 5.5 % of body weight. The group aiming for 0.7 % a week gained 2.1 % lean mass. The group aiming for 1.4 % a week kept the same lean mass. A 625 kcal deficit removes about 1.25 lb (0.57 kg) a week, which is 0.7 % of a 180 lb (81.6 kg) body.

What if you are coming back after a break?

Pick the silhouette that matches the mirror today, and the numbers follow from it. IronQuest reads your current body, so a returning lifter at 20 % lands on the same 625 kcal deficit as the other 2 cases. If the break left you soft and narrow, the skinny-fat recomp fits. If you kept most of your size, the cut fits.

The muscle you built before comes back faster than it came the first time. Seaborne and colleagues trained 8 untrained men for 7 weeks, stopped them for 7 weeks, then trained them again for 7 weeks, in Scientific Reports in 2018. Leg lean mass rose 6.5 % in the first block and fell back near baseline during the break. The second block ended 12.4 % above baseline, a larger gain than the first block. The trial was small, and the 7 men measured to the end were beginners when it started, so read it as a direction for returning lifters.

How many weeks from 20 % to 15 % and to 10 %?

About 13 to 16 weeks to 15 % and 29 to 37 weeks to 10 % for men of 160 to 200 lb (72.6 to 90.7 kg). The table runs IronQuest's projection at 7,500 to 9,999 steps a day. Each week, the projection reads body fat again, which moves the deficit to 450 kcal once you drop under 20 %, to 350 kcal under 15 % and to 250 kcal under 12 %. It counts 7,700 kcal per kg of body weight lost, with 15 % of every kilo coming from lean mass.

What does the plan look like for a 168 lb man at 22 %?

He eats 1,950 kcal a day and 154 g of protein. Take a man of 168 lb (76 kg) at 22 % body fat who walks 7,500 to 9,999 steps a day:

  1. Lean mass: 76 × 0.78 = 59.3 kg (130.7 lb).
  2. Basal metabolic rate with Katch-McArdle: 370 + 21.6 × 59.3 = 1,650 kcal.
  3. Maintenance: 1,650 × 1.55 = 2,558 kcal, shown as 2,550 kcal.
  4. Target: 2,558 − 625 = 1,933 kcal, rounded to 1,950 kcal.
  5. Protein: 2.6 × 59.3 = 154 g a day.

The projection puts him at 15 % in about 17 weeks, at 151 lb (68.4 kg). He reaches 10 % in about 33 weeks, at 141 lb (63.8 kg). In the app, the weekly check-in reads his average weight for the week and moves the target. After 2 weekly reviews in a row without a drop, IronQuest takes 200 kcal more off the day, up to 400 kcal, never under his basal metabolic rate. His 3 gym sessions take 125 min a week, set by set in the 3-day workout program.

Who should not use these numbers?

Women should not use them. The bands, the 625 kcal deficit and the 10 % finish line are IronQuest's rules for men. A man whose 20 % comes from a body-mass-index formula should treat it as a rough reading: the Deurenberg formula reads muscle as fat, so a trained lifter's estimate can run high. If your job has you lifting boxes all day, steps miss part of your activity, and your real maintenance sits above the table's.

Questions

What changes at 18 % body fat?

At 18 % the deficit drops to 450 kcal a day, because 15 to 20 % is a smaller band in IronQuest. A 180 lb (81.6 kg) man at 18 % eats 2,350 kcal a day, reaches 15 % in about 9 weeks and 10 % in about 28 weeks.

How fast does the scale drop at 20 % body fat?

At 625 kcal a day, the deficit adds up to 4,375 kcal a week, about 1.25 lb (0.57 kg) of body weight. For a 180 lb (81.6 kg) man, that is 0.7 % of body weight a week, inside the 0.5 to 1 % range Helms, Aragon and Fitschen give natural bodybuilders dieting for a contest.

Do daily steps change the calories at 20 % body fat?

Steps change maintenance, so they change the daily target. A 180 lb (81.6 kg) man at 20 % eats 2,150 kcal a day at 7,500 to 9,999 steps and 2,450 kcal from 10,000 steps. Under 5,000 steps, the target can't go under his basal metabolic rate of about 1,780 kcal, so the deficit shrinks to about 356 kcal.
