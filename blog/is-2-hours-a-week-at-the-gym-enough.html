Is 2 hours a week at the gym enough to build muscle?

Yes, 2 hours a week at the gym is enough to build muscle if the time goes into hard sets with timed rests. Yes, 2 hours a week is enough to build muscle, as long as those 120 minutes go into hard sets and the rests are timed. IronQuest's 3-session program fits 60 working sets into 125 min, and that gives the back, chest and side delts 9 sets a week each. The research floor sits at 4 weekly sets per muscle. Where 2 hours runs short is the lifter who wants 10 or more sets on every muscle, legs included, because the clock doesn't stretch that far.

How many sets fit in 2 hours a week?

About 60 working sets fit in 2 hours, at a little over 2 minutes per set with the rest included. That pace comes from the 3-day workout program IronQuest runs: 20 exercises, 3 sets each, over 3 sessions of 44, 43 and 38 minutes, laid out below day by day.

The rests keep the week at 125 minutes. The incline, bench and overhead presses, the weighted pull-ups and the first leg lift get 2:00 of rest. The rear-delt raise, the Boulder Shoulders upright row, the calf raises and the wrist curls get 1:00, and every other slot gets 1:30. Rest that nobody times runs long, and then the same 60 sets stop fitting in 2 hours.

How much volume does a muscle need to grow?

At least 4 hard sets a week per muscle, according to the 2021 review by Iversen, Norum, Schoenfeld and Fimland on time-efficient training. They wrote it for people who say they have no time to lift, and they set that floor with loads you can lift 6 to 15 times. More sets still bring more growth above the floor.

The 2017 meta-analysis by Schoenfeld, Ogborn and Krieger pooled 34 training groups from 15 studies. Each extra weekly set per muscle went with a muscle gain 0.37 percentage points higher. The authors sorted volume into 3 bands: fewer than 5, 5 to 9, and 10 or more sets a week. The 10-set band showed the largest gains, but the split between bands only reached a trend. So read 4 sets as the floor and 10 sets as a good target, and plan your 2 hours between the two.

Is 2 hours a week enough to get stronger?

Yes, and strength needs less of the 2 hours than size does. The 2020 meta-analysis by Androulakis-Korakakis, Fisher and Steele looked for the smallest dose that still raises a 1-rep max in trained men. In the lowest-dose groups of 5 studies, a single set done 1 to 3 times a week raised the squat by about 17.5 kg and the bench press by about 8.3 kg. The authors call those gains suboptimal but significant. Their advice for a minimum is 1 set of 6 to 12 reps at about 70 to 85 % of the max, 2 to 3 times a week for 8 to 12 weeks.

The 2019 trial by Schoenfeld and colleagues points the same way. 34 trained men did 1, 3 or 5 sets per exercise, 3 times a week, for 8 weeks. Squat and bench press strength rose by similar amounts in all 3 groups, and the low-volume sessions took 13 minutes. Muscle size grew more with more sets. So if your 2 hours are aimed at size, spend them on sets, and the strength comes along.

Where does 2 hours a week stop being enough?

2 hours stops being enough when you want 10 or more sets on every muscle. At the program's pace of about 2 minutes per set, taking the quads and hamstrings from 6 to 10 sets each adds 8 sets, around 17 minutes a week. Doing the same for every muscle in the table runs well past 3 hours.

It also falls short in these cases:

  • If bigger legs come first for you. IronQuest gives the legs 1 hard session a week, because its target is the shoulder-to-waist ratio.
  • If growth has stalled on about 10 sets a muscle. In the 2019 trial, the 5-set group did 30 to 45 weekly sets per muscle and grew more than the 1-set group at 3 of the 4 sites measured. That volume doesn't fit in 120 minutes.
  • If you want the health guidelines covered in the gym too. The 2020 WHO guidelines ask for 150 to 300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity a week on top of strength work on 2 or more days.
  • If your rests aren't timed. 125 minutes holds only with 1:00 to 2:00 of rest after each set or superset round.

The 2019 and 2020 papers measured trained men only, so they say less about women or about lifters in their first months.

Questions

Is 1 hour a week at the gym enough?

For strength, often yes. In a 2019 trial by Schoenfeld and colleagues, trained men doing 3 sessions of 13 min a week gained similar squat and bench press strength to the higher-volume groups. Their muscles grew less than those of the men doing 5 sets per exercise at 3 of the 4 sites measured.

Should 2 hours be split into 2 sessions or 3?

Split it into 3 sessions of about 40 min. A 2016 meta-analysis by Schoenfeld, Ogborn and Krieger found more growth when a muscle is trained twice a week than once. IronQuest's 3 sessions train the chest and side delts twice a week and the back 3 times.

Does walking count toward the 2 hours?

No. The 125 min of IronQuest are lifting only. The World Health Organization counts aerobic activity apart, at 150 to 300 min of moderate effort a week. IronQuest has no cardio sessions and pays XP for daily steps, at every milestone from 2,500 to 20,000 steps.
