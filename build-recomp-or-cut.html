How the calculator decides

The calculator runs the same rules as the IronQuest app. A skinny body builds. A skinny-fat body runs a recomp, and a body that is muscular but fat, or overweight, runs a cut. If you type your body fat instead, 15 % or under builds, 15 to 25 % is a recomp and 25 % and above is a cut. A recomp and a cut use the same math: a deficit set by your body fat until you reach 10 %, then maintenance calories and a build.

Maintenance comes from the Katch-McArdle formula, which works from lean mass: basal metabolic rate (BMR) = 370 + 21.6 × lean mass in kg. That BMR is multiplied by an activity factor read from your daily steps: 1.2 under 5,000 steps, 1.375 up to 7,499, 1.55 up to 9,999 and 1.725 from 10,000. The target is maintenance minus the deficit, never under your BMR, rounded to 50 kcal.

Protein, fat and carbs

Protein is set per kg of lean mass: 2.6 g on a cut, inside the 2.3 to 3.1 g that Helms, Aragon and Fitschen give for lifters in a deficit, and 2.2 g on a build, the upper bound in the meta-analysis by Morton and colleagues. Fat takes 25 % of the daily calories at 9 kcal per gram, and carbs fill the rest at 4 kcal per gram.

A worked example

A man of 82 kg at 22 % body fat who walks 7,000 steps a day:

  1. Lean mass: 82 × 0.78 = 64.0 kg.
  2. BMR: 370 + 21.6 × 64.0 = 1,752 kcal.
  3. Maintenance: 1,752 × 1.375 = 2,408 kcal, shown as 2,400 kcal.
  4. 22 % sits in the 20 to 25 % band, so the deficit is 625 kcal.
  5. Target: 2,408 − 625 = 1,783 kcal, rounded to 1,800 kcal.
  6. Protein: 2.6 × 64.0 = 166 g, with 50 g of fat and 172 g of carbs.

The projection runs those rules day by day, counts 7,700 kcal per kg of body fat and 15 % of every kilo lost as lean mass, and reads the deficit again every 7 days. He reaches 10 % body fat in 36 weeks, at about 68.9 kg.

Limits

  • The body-fat bands, the deficits and the 10 % finish line apply to men. For women, the calculator gives maintenance calories and a protein target.
  • The body-type estimate uses the Deurenberg formula from BMI and age, which reads muscle as fat. That is why the result stays inside the range of the body type you pick. A smart scale or a tape estimate can be off by a few points too.
  • The weeks assume you eat the target every day. In the app, a weekly check-in reads your average weight and moves the target when the scale stalls.
  • Steps miss a physical job. If you lift boxes all day, your maintenance is higher than the result.

Questions

Should I bulk or cut if I am skinny-fat?

IronQuest puts a skinny-fat body on a recomp. You start under maintenance, by 450 kcal a day between 18 and 20 % body fat and by 625 kcal between 20 and 25 %, with 2.6 g of protein per kg of lean mass and 3 gym sessions a week. At 10 % body fat the plan moves to maintenance calories and builds muscle.

At what body fat should I stop cutting?

At 10 %. The first weekly review that reads your body fat at 10 % or under ends the cut, and your target becomes the maintenance calories of that week.

How big should my calorie deficit be?

It follows your body fat: 875 kcal a day at 25 % and above, 625 kcal from 20 %, 450 kcal from 15 %, 350 kcal from 12 % and 250 kcal under 12 %. The day never goes under your basal metabolic rate.

Does a build mean eating in a surplus?

It starts at maintenance. If your weekly average weight has not gone up for 2 reviews in a row, IronQuest adds 200 kcal a day, up to 600 kcal above maintenance.

What happens when my weight stops dropping on a cut?

After 2 weekly reviews in a row without a drop, IronQuest takes 200 kcal more off your day, 400 kcal at most. The target still never goes under your basal metabolic rate.
