Build, recomp or cut: which one for your body fat

IronQuest picks the protocol from your body type: build if you are skinny, recomp if you are skinny-fat, cut if you are muscular but fat or overweight. On a recomp or a cut you eat 250 to 875 kcal under maintenance a day, depending on your body fat, until you reach 10 % body fat, and then the plan switches to building muscle.

Updated November 16, 2026

Build
Skinny body. Maintenance calories from day 1, 2.2 g of protein per kg of lean mass.
Recomp
Skinny-fat body. Deficit set by body fat until 10 %, then build.
Cut
Muscular but fat, or overweight. Deficit set by body fat until 10 %, then build.
Review
Every week, from your average weight, your body fat and your steps.

Which protocol for which body

During setup you pick the body that looks like yours among 4 silhouettes. That choice sets the protocol and the range of body fat IronQuest reads you in.

Your starting body fat comes from the Deurenberg formula for men (1991): 1.2 × BMI + 0.23 × age − 16.2, in percent. The result is then kept inside the range of the body type you picked. BMI reads a muscular man as fat, so the silhouette you chose has the last word.

A recomp and a cut run on the same rule. Both start under maintenance, and the size of the deficit comes from your body fat, as the table further down shows. All the formulas on this page are the versions for men.

How maintenance calories are set

IronQuest uses the Katch-McArdle formula, which works from lean mass. Two men of 90 kg, one at 15 % body fat and one at 30 %, get different numbers.

  1. Lean mass = weight × (1 − body fat).
  2. Basal metabolic rate (BMR) = 370 + 21.6 × lean mass in kg.
  3. Maintenance = BMR × activity factor, rounded to 50 kcal.

The activity factor comes from your average daily steps, read from Apple Health. Without step data the factor is 1.55, which stands for 10,000 steps a day and 3 sessions a week.

The deficit for each body fat

On a recomp or a cut, the daily target is maintenance minus a deficit. Each body-fat band has a range, and IronQuest applies the middle of it. The more fat you carry, the larger the deficit.

20 % to under 25 %500 to 750 kcal625 kcal
15 % to under 20 %400 to 500 kcal450 kcal

Under 12 %200 to 300 kcal250 kcal

One floor applies to every band: the daily target never goes under your BMR. The band is read again at every weekly review, so the deficit shrinks as you get leaner. The target is rounded to 50 kcal.

A worked example

Take a man of 115.5 kg at 31 % body fat, with the default activity factor of 1.55. His protocol is a cut.

  1. Lean mass: 115.5 × 0.69 = 79.7 kg.
  2. BMR: 370 + 21.6 × 79.7 = 2,091 kcal.
  3. Maintenance: 2,091 × 1.55 = 3,242 kcal, shown as 3,250 kcal.
  4. Deficit at 31 % body fat: 875 kcal.
  5. Daily target: 3,242 − 875 = 2,367 kcal, rounded to 2,350 kcal.

His macros on that target are 207 g of protein, 65 g of fat and 234 g of carbs.

Protein, fat and carbs

Protein is set per kg of lean mass: 2.6 g on a cut and 2.2 g on a build. The cut figure sits inside the 2.3 to 3.1 g per kg of lean mass that Helms, Aragon and Fitschen recommend in a deficit (Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition, 2014). The build figure uses the 2.2 g upper bound reported by Morton and colleagues (British Journal of Sports Medicine, 2018).

Fat takes 25 % of the daily calories, at 9 kcal per gram. Carbs fill what is left, at 4 kcal per gram, the same as protein.

The weekly review

Once a week IronQuest opens a check-in: a weigh-in and 4 tape measurements. The review then reads 3 things.

  • Weight. The average of your weigh-ins over the last 7 days. With no weigh-in that week, the target holds.
  • Body fat. The value you log at the check-in, estimated from your height and belly measurement. If you log none, IronQuest moves your last body fat with your weight and counts 15 % of every kilo lost as lean mass.
  • Steps. The average of the 7 days before the review sets the activity factor.

The target is then recalculated with the formulas above. Two more rules cover a scale that stops moving. On a cut, 2 reviews in a row without a drop take 200 kcal more off the day, and 400 kcal at most. On a build, 2 reviews in a row without a rise add 200 kcal, up to 600 kcal above maintenance.

Cut until 10 % body fat, then build

The first review that reads your body fat at 10 % or under ends the cut. Your target becomes that week's maintenance calories, protein goes from 2.6 to 2.2 g per kg of lean mass, and the build rule takes over. The shoulder measurement to build toward is in the golden ratio calculator.

The 12-month projection

Before you start, IronQuest runs the same rules day by day over 365 days and draws the weight curve you then follow. The projection reads the deficit again every 7 days, counts 7,700 kcal for each kilo of body fat and 15 % of the weight lost as lean mass. Once the body reaches 10 % body fat, or from day 1 on a build, it counts 0.15 kg of lean mass gained per week at maintenance.

The training side of the plan is 3 sessions and 125 minutes a week, laid out in the 3-day gym program.

Questions

Should I bulk or cut if I am skinny-fat?

IronQuest puts a skinny-fat body on the recomp protocol. You start under maintenance, by 450 kcal a day between 18 and 20 % body fat and by 625 kcal between 20 and 25 %, with 2.6 g of protein per kg of lean mass and 3 gym sessions a week. At 10 % body fat the plan moves to maintenance calories and builds muscle.

At what body fat should I stop cutting?

At 10 %. The first weekly review that reads your body fat at 10 % or under ends the cut, and your target becomes the maintenance calories of that week.

How big should my calorie deficit be?

It follows your body fat: 875 kcal a day at 25 % and above, 625 kcal from 20 %, 450 kcal from 15 %, 350 kcal from 12 % and 250 kcal under 12 %. The day never goes under your basal metabolic rate.

How much protein do I need on a cut and on a build?

2.6 g per kg of lean mass on a cut and 2.2 g per kg of lean mass on a build. At 80 kg and 20 % body fat your lean mass is 64 kg, which gives 166 g of protein a day on a cut and 141 g on a build.

Does a build mean eating in a surplus?

It starts at maintenance. If your weekly average weight has not gone up for 2 reviews in a row, IronQuest adds 200 kcal a day, up to 600 kcal above maintenance.

What happens when my weight stops dropping on a cut?

After 2 weekly reviews in a row without a drop, IronQuest takes 200 kcal more off your day, and 400 kcal at most. The target still never goes under your basal metabolic rate.
