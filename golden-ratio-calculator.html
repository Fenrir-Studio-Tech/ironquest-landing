What the golden ratio body is

The golden ratio is the number 1.618. Applied to a body, it compares two tape measurements: the shoulders, taken around the deltoids, and the waist, taken at its narrowest point. At 1.618 the shoulders are a little over one and a half times the waist, which reads as a V from the front and from the back.

The ratio alone says nothing about size. A waist of 100 cm with shoulders of 161.8 cm scores 1.618 as well, so IronQuest anchors the waist to your height first.

How to measure your waist and shoulders

Use a soft tape and stand relaxed, arms at your sides. Keep the tape flat against the skin and level with the floor all the way around. Breathe out normally and read the number without pulling the tape tight.

  • Waist. At the narrowest point of the waist, usually above the navel.
  • Shoulders. Around the deltoids, where the shoulders are widest. The tape passes over the upper chest and the upper back, so a second person or a mirror helps to keep it level.

Measure once a week, on the same day, before you eat. Food and water move the waist reading through the day, so the morning number is the one to compare from week to week.

How training moves the shoulder number

The shoulder tape goes around the deltoids, the upper chest and the upper back, so it grows with all three. In the IronQuest program the lateral raises, the upright rows and the rear-delt raises add width to the deltoids. The overhead press, the incline press and the weighted pull-ups add thickness in front and behind.

The whole program is 3 sessions and 125 minutes a week, and 2 of the 3 sessions train the upper body. Every exercise, set and rest is in the 3-day gym program for a V-shaped body.

How diet moves the waist number

Your waist follows your body fat. If you carry fat, the waist comes down on a cut: you eat under your maintenance calories, by 250 to 875 kcal a day depending on how much fat you carry, until you reach 10 % body fat. If you are already lean, the waist stays where it is and the work is on the shoulders.

The rules behind the calorie target are in build, recomp or cut.

How IronQuest tracks it every week

Once a week the app opens a check-in. You weigh in, then take 4 tape measurements: belly at the navel, waist, shoulders and thigh. The result screen puts your waist and shoulders next to the golden numbers for your height, so you read both gaps at once.

The belly measurement gives a body-fat estimate with the Relative Fat Mass formula for men (Woolcott and Bergman, Scientific Reports, 2018): 64 − 20 × height ÷ belly. The check-in then recalculates your daily calorie target from that week's weight, body fat and steps. Weight, body fat and the tape measurements sit on one chart against the 12-month plan.

Questions

What is the golden ratio for shoulders to waist?

It is 1.618. Divide your shoulder circumference by your waist circumference, both taken with a tape. A waist of 80 cm calls for shoulders of 129.4 cm.

What shoulder measurement matches a 32-inch waist?

A 32-inch waist calls for 51.8 inches around the shoulders (32 × 1.618). A 34-inch waist calls for 55.0 inches.

What is the ideal waist for my height?

IronQuest sets the golden waist at 0.447 times your height. That gives 80.5 cm for a height of 180 cm, and 31.3 inches for a height of 70 inches (5 ft 10).

Is the ratio measured with a circumference or with shoulder width?

With circumferences. The tape goes around the deltoids where the shoulders are widest, and around the waist at its narrowest point. A shoulder width taken straight across from bone to bone gives a much smaller number and does not work with 1.618.

Can I reach the golden ratio by losing fat alone?

Yes, if your shoulders already measure 1.618 times your golden waist. Enter your height in the calculator and compare your shoulders with the golden shoulders. If they match, only the waist has to come down, and that is a matter of body fat.

How long does it take to reach the golden ratio?

It depends on the two gaps the calculator gives you. IronQuest plans over 12 months: it reads your height, weight and body type, projects the body you can reach in a year, and shows your waist and shoulders against the golden numbers at every weekly check-in.
