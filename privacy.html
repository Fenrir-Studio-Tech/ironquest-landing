1. Data We Collect

Account data

You start IronQuest on an anonymous session, identified by an ID with no email address attached. When you sign up with Apple or Google, we receive your email address and, if the provider shares them, your name and profile picture. We store the tokens the provider issues for your account. We never see a password: it stays with Apple or Google. Each session record also holds your IP address and your device's user agent.

Profile data

The onboarding questionnaire asks for your nickname, avatar, goal, training experience, age, height, weight, body type, estimated body fat, training days, whether you can do a pull-up, your preferred lower body lift, any injuries (back, knees, shoulders, wrists), dinner time, units, and how you heard about IronQuest, along with your yes or no answers to 3 statements about your habits. Your answers reach our servers during the questionnaire, before you create an account. We use them to set up your program and calculate your calorie and macro targets, using fixed formulas and no AI.

Workout data

We store your workout programs, routines, sessions, sets, reps, weights, distances, the effort rating you add to a set (RPE), and your personal records. We also store the XP, rank, streak, achievements, and quests they earn you.

Nutrition and measurement data

We store your nutrition plan and targets, the foods and portions you log, your check-ins, the recipes you save or edit, and the TikTok links you import. We also store the body measurements you log in the app, such as weight, body fat, and waist.

Health data (HealthKit)

With your explicit permission, IronQuest reads the following data from Apple HealthKit on iOS: step count, body weight, body fat percentage, lean body mass, height, and waist circumference. This data is synced to our servers alongside your other workout data to power your daily activity tracking, body-composition history, progression XP, and cross-device sync.

IronQuest also writes back to Apple Health the body measurements you log inside the app (weight, body fat percentage, lean body mass, and waist circumference), so your data stays consistent across your other health and fitness apps. Step count and height are read-only; we never write them. You can revoke HealthKit access at any time in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Health → IronQuest.

Progress photos

If you choose to use progress photos, we store them in encrypted form, along with the pose and date of each one. Section 5 covers them in full.

Device data

We collect your device identifier, platform (iOS or Android), push notification token, notification permission status, time zone, language, and app version. We also store your notification preferences, training days, and eating window, which set when your reminders go out.

Usage and advertising data

The app records what you do in it and can record your screen for product analytics, and it reports your install and certain actions for advertising measurement. Section 6 lists this data.

Server logs

Our API logs each request: its method, URL, status, response time, user agent, origin, query parameters, and body, plus the first 1,000 characters of the response. Fields named like passwords, tokens, and keys are masked, and the progress photo routes log no bodies. Other request bodies are logged as sent, so these logs can contain workout, nutrition, and body data.

Subscription data

RevenueCat manages your subscription status, plan, trial, and purchase history through the App Store or Google Play. We do not collect or store payment details (credit card numbers, billing addresses) directly.

Website and support messages

The ironquest.app website runs no analytics and sets no cookies. Its contact form opens your email app with your message, and we receive what you then send to contact@fenrirstudio.io.

3. AI Processing: TikTok Recipe Import

The recipe import is the only feature of IronQuest that uses AI. It runs when you paste or share a TikTok link in the app and turns that post into a recipe in your account. Your phone sends the link to our servers, and our servers call these providers:

  • Apify receives the post's address and returns its caption, cover image, subtitles, and video or slides. TikTok itself answers our servers' requests for the short link and the post's public details.
  • Google (Gemini) reads the video or slides for spoken and on-screen text. It then generates a photo of the dish from the recipe's title, ingredients, and plating, and from the cover.
  • Anthropic (Claude) extracts the recipe from the caption, cover, transcript, on-screen text, and creator's handle, and matches the ingredients to a food database.
  • Amazon Web Services stores the cover and the generated photo in a private S3 bucket in Frankfurt, Germany (eu-central-1).

These providers receive the content of the TikTok post and the recipe made from it. They do not receive your account ID, email address, body data, or food log. We remove the sharing codes TikTok adds to a link before we forward it. A public post can show or name the people who made it, including their handle.

Your record of an import stays until 30 days after the import ends. The recipe stays until you delete it or your account. What we extracted from the video is tied to the video and to no player, so it stays after you delete your account and serves other players who import the same post.

4. Apple HealthKit

On iOS, IronQuest reads the following data from Apple HealthKit: step count, body weight, body fat percentage, lean body mass, height, and waist circumference. IronQuest also writes back the body measurements you log inside the app (weight, body fat percentage, lean body mass, and waist circumference) so they stay in sync with your other health and fitness apps. Step count and height are read-only; we never write them. We commit to the following:

  • HealthKit data is never used for advertising, marketing, or similar services.
  • HealthKit data is never sold, shared, or disclosed to third parties for advertising.
  • HealthKit data is never sent to Anthropic, Google, Apify, AppsFlyer, Meta, TikTok, or RevenueCat.
  • Our servers run on Heroku and our database on Aiven, so these two providers host the HealthKit data we store.
  • When you share your steps card, PostHog, our product analytics provider, receives the figures on it: steps, best day, and days at your goal. PostHog receives no other HealthKit values.
  • Health data read from HealthKit (steps, weight, body fat percentage, lean body mass, height, waist circumference) is stored on our servers solely to power your daily activity tracking, body-composition history, progression XP, and cross-device sync.
  • Measurements written back to Apple Health are limited to values you explicitly log inside IronQuest (weight, body fat percentage, lean body mass, waist circumference). Once written, those entries live in your HealthKit store and are managed by you through the Apple Health app.
  • You can revoke HealthKit permissions at any time through iOS Settings, and you can delete all health data stored on our servers by deleting your account (see section 9).

5. Progress Photos

Progress photos are optional, and the rest of IronQuest works without them. You take them with the camera inside the app or choose them from your photo library, on the measurements screen or during a check-in. We treat a body photo stored next to your weight and body fat as health data, and we process it on the basis of your explicit consent.

Age and consent

Progress photos are for players aged 18 and over, a higher limit than the one for the app as a whole (see section 10). We read the age you gave during onboarding, add the years since, and hide the feature when the result is under 18. We do not check that age against an identity document, and an account with no age on record keeps access.

The first time you use photos, a screen explains how they are handled. Tapping GOT IT gives your consent. We record the consent version and its date on our servers, at your next connection if you are offline. You withdraw consent by deleting your photos. The consent record stays until you delete your account.

On your phone

The app re-encodes every photo on your phone before it saves or uploads it, which removes location and device metadata such as GPS coordinates and the camera make and model. The date the photo was taken is read first and kept, so your photos sort by date. A photo you choose from your library stays there unchanged.

Each photo is encrypted on your phone with AES-256-GCM before upload, using a key specific to your account, and only the encrypted files leave the phone. A photo still waiting to upload and the copies the app opens to show you sit in the app's storage on your phone without this encryption. Signing out removes those files and the key from the phone. It gives waiting photos up to 5 seconds to finish uploading, and a photo that has not finished by then is lost.

If your phone has a passcode, a lock using Face ID, Touch ID, or the passcode is on by default, and you can turn it off. It closes again 60 seconds after the app leaves the foreground.

On our servers

The encrypted files are stored in a private Amazon Web Services (AWS) S3 bucket in Frankfurt, Germany (eu-central-1). Your encryption key is generated by AWS Key Management Service (KMS) in the same region, and our database keeps it only in a form encrypted by a master key that KMS protects. When a phone signed in to your account needs the key, our server asks KMS to decrypt it and sends it to that phone over an encrypted connection. Our servers can therefore obtain your key, and this is what lets your photos reach a new phone if you lose the old one.

Our database also holds, for each photo, an identifier, the pose, the date taken, whether it came from a check-in, whether it finished uploading, and any deletion date. It also holds a daily count of your uploads.

How photos are used

We use your photos for one purpose: showing them to you in the app. We do not use them to train any model, analyze their content, or advertise, and we do not share them with anyone. The encrypted files go straight from your phone to storage, and our servers never open an image.

On your phone, the app does analyze the outline of your body, in the camera view and in your saved photos, to help you frame and line up your photos, and that outline never leaves your phone.

No one at IronQuest looks at your photos, and no admin tool displays a photo or reads a key. AWS logs each use of the master key. Our product analytics provider receives how many photos you saved or deleted and their poses, never an image. Session recording stops on every screen that shows a photo or the camera, and those areas are masked in the recording.

You can send yourself a copy of a photo with Save a copy, which opens your phone's share sheet. Where that copy goes is your choice, and this policy no longer covers it once it leaves the app.

Deleting photos

You can delete one photo or all of them in the app at any time. They leave your phone at once. The deletion reaches our servers at your next sync, and we then remove the encrypted files from storage. If storage is unreachable we keep retrying, which can take up to a few days. The identifier, pose, and date of a deleted photo stay in your account history until you delete your account.

Deleting your account removes every encrypted photo file first, then your encrypted key and the data listed in section 9. If storage cannot be reached at that moment, the deletion stops, your account stays, and you can try again.

Product analytics (PostHog)

Store installs send product analytics to PostHog's EU cloud from the first launch, under an anonymous ID and then under your account ID once you sign up. PostHog receives these onboarding answers: how you heard about IronQuest, goal, experience, your exact age, body type, your answers to the 3 statements, training days, whether you can do a pull-up, lower body lift, injuries, dinner time, and briefing time. It also receives your units, language, chosen protocol, install source and campaign, the permissions you granted, and the screens you view.

After onboarding it receives events about what you do: offers seen and purchases, including price; sessions completed, with the number of sets, records, and RPE ratings; foods logged, with their source and never the food itself; check-ins, with whether you weighed in or logged body fat, and how many tape measures and photos they include; changes to your calorie target; achievements, quests, shares, recipe imports, notifications opened, and app errors. RevenueCat sends PostHog your subscription events. These events never carry your nickname, avatar, height, weight, or body fat.

Session recording is on for every session on store installs. It captures your screen as you use the app, so a recording can show your workouts, nutrition, and body figures. Text you type is masked and images are not, apart from the photo screens described in section 5. Recordings are kept for 30 days.

Advertising measurement (AppsFlyer, Meta, TikTok)

AppsFlyer is the only advertising SDK in the app. It links your install to the ad or link that brought you, and it receives your account ID and these events:

  • App opened*
  • The Quest Accepted achievement, at the end of the commitment step*
  • The end of the onboarding questionnaire*
  • Sign-up, with the method (Apple or Google)*
  • The store sheet opening, with the offer and price*
  • A free trial or subscription starting, with the offer*
  • Exercises and program templates you view
  • The words you type in the exercise search
  • First workout and program achievements, and rank levels reached

AppsFlyer relays the events marked * to Meta and TikTok, which use them to measure and optimize our ads. RevenueCat sends AppsFlyer every purchase and renewal with its revenue. None of these events carries a questionnaire answer, a body measurement, HealthKit data, or a photo.

On iOS, the app asks your permission to track when you tap GET STARTED. If you allow it, AppsFlyer and RevenueCat receive your advertising identifier (IDFA). If you decline, the app keeps sending the same events to AppsFlyer without the IDFA, using AppsFlyer's install identifier and the vendor identifier (IDFV), and AppsFlyer still relays the marked events to Meta and TikTok. On Android, the app shows no such prompt.

7. Local Data Storage

IronQuest is an offline-first app. Your workout data is stored locally on your device in a SQLite database, so you can log a session without an internet connection. Signing in, buying a subscription, importing a recipe, uploading a photo, and deleting your account all need one.

When connectivity is available, your local data syncs with our servers to enable backup and multi-device access. The sync process appends each change to an event store, which keeps your full history until you delete your account.

Progress photo files sit outside this database and leave your device only in encrypted form (see section 5).

8. Data Retention

We retain your data for as long as your account is active. If you delete your account, we delete your data as described in section 9.

An onboarding you start without signing up stays on our servers under its anonymous ID, with no automatic deletion date.

Section 3 gives the retention of recipe imports, and section 6 that of session recordings. Server logs and database backups expire on the schedules of Heroku and Aiven.

We keep your progress photos until you delete them or delete your account (see section 5).

9. Data Deletion

You can delete your account and associated data from within the app. When you request deletion, we remove:

  • All your events: programs, sessions, sets, performance data, food logs, check-ins, and measurements
  • Health metrics read from Apple HealthKit and stored on our servers (steps, weight, body fat %, lean body mass, height, waist circumference)
  • Your onboarding answers, including those held under the anonymous session you started from
  • Your personal recipes and recipe import records
  • Device registrations, push notification tokens, notification settings, and planned notifications
  • Custom workout programs you authored
  • Your authentication record, sessions, and linked Apple or Google account. We also ask Apple or Google to revoke access, and the deletion goes ahead if that request fails
  • Progress photos: the encrypted files in storage, your encrypted photo key, your consent record, and the photo records in your history
  • The app's data on the phone you delete from

Measurements that IronQuest has previously written to Apple Health (weight, body fat %, lean body mass, waist) remain in your HealthKit store after account deletion and are managed by you through the Apple Health app. To fully cancel a subscription, use the App Store or Google Play settings on your device.

Deleting your account does not reach the data already held by PostHog, AppsFlyer, Meta, TikTok, or RevenueCat. The app stops tying new AppsFlyer events to you on that phone. To request erasure of what these providers hold, write to us (see section 11).

10. Children's Privacy

IronQuest is not directed at children under 16 years of age. We do not knowingly collect personal data from anyone under 16. The onboarding age picker starts at 16, and we do not check the age you choose. If you believe a child under 16 has provided us with personal data, please contact us and we will delete it.

Progress photos have a higher limit: they are for players aged 18 and over (see section 5).

12. Security

Data travels over encrypted connections (TLS), and the app keeps your sign-in token in your phone's secure storage. Only one operator account can sign in to our admin tool, which shows account details and each player's activity, such as sessions and programs. Apart from progress photos, the app does not encrypt your data before sending it, so our servers can read it. No system is fully secure, and we cannot guarantee absolute security. Section 5 describes the protections specific to progress photos.
